PDCE The PDCE LIGHTNING PROTECTION SYSTEM is a lightning protection device designed to suppress lightning strikes.

Due to the effects of climate change, lightning is no longer a region-specific risk; it can occur almost anywhere. The focus is no longer on responding after a lightning strike, but on preventing damage before it occurs.

Conventional lightning rods are designed to actively attract lightning, which can result in strike-related damage to surrounding facilities and electronic equipment.

Developed from engineers’ desire to eliminate lightning damage itself, the PDCE LIGHTNING PROTECTION SYSTEM is designed not to attract lightning, but to reduce the likelihood of a protected structure becoming a lightning target.

Misconception 1：Lightning strikes high places

This is a mistake As a result, lightning often strikes high places, but height is not a factor in lightning strikes. This is because lightning cannot determine the height of structures on the ground above 7,000m. Therefore, lightning strikes not depending on the height, but on the strength of the electrification and the discharge from the ground.

Misconception 2：Lightning strikes one way

This is also a mistake The electrification strength of the earth's surface is not uniform, and there are conditions such as "difficult to discharge" and "easiness to discharge" from the charged part. Therefore, it occurs due to the correlation between thunderclouds and the surface of the earth.

PDCE LIGHTNING PROTECTION SYSTEM

Even if a thundercloud approaches, if no "welcoming discharge" is generated, a discharge path will not be formed. Therefore, based on the completely opposite idea of the conventional lightning rod, a negative electrode is generated at the top of the building to prevent discharge from the lightning rod. This lightning rod suppresses lightning strikes. However, even if this can prevent direct lightning strikes, if a lightning strike occurs outside of this effective range, the induced voltage will enter the building via wires. To prevent this, a separate surge protection device is required.

conventional lightning rod

The principle of lightning strikes (in the case of summer lightning) is that a "leader discharge" starts from the thundercloud to the ground at first. After that, small-scale discharges begin (“welcoming discharges”) in search of paths that facilitate discharges from the ground toward the bottom of the thundercloud. When the discharge from above and the discharge from below join hands, a large discharge is added. This is a lightning strike. Conventional lightning rods guide lightning to the lightning rod at the top of the building, etc., to prevent lightning from striking the side of the building without permission, and discharge energy to the ground to ensure safety inside the building

FAQ

How is this different from a conventional lightning rod?

Conventional lightning rods operate on the principle of “capturing and diverting lightning,” whereas the PDCE LIGHTNING PROTECTION SYSTEM is based on the concept of “controlling the electric field to prevent lightning from occurring.”
This helps reduce side effects (induced lightning damage) to electronic devices and communication equipment commonly found in modern buildings.

• Conventional lightning rods were invented about 260 years ago; at that time, during the era of oil lamps, the inductive method was sufficient.

• However, today’s power and communication networks are complex, posing a risk of damage to equipment due to induced currents.While lightning does not always strike a lightning protection device, the PDCE LIGHTNING PROTECTION SYSTEM safeguards the protected area and, in the unlikely event of a strike, safely diverts the current to the ground through a grounding system.

Examples of damage with conventional lightning rods

  1. Lightning struck a lighting tower, damaging the lighting control module [Stadium in Shizuoka Prefecture]
  2. Lightning struck a three-story pagoda, causing a fire to break out from the internal fire alarm system [Important Cultural Property in Gifu Prefecture]
  3. Lightning struck a radio tower, damaging home appliances in nearby residences [Ishikawa Prefecture]
  4. Lightning struck a ship, damaging the radar and gyrocompass [Shipping Company]

Since electrical equipment is indispensable in modern life, preventing these accidents requires preventing the flow of high currents in the first place. The PDCE LIGHTNING PROTECTION SYSTEM safeguards the protected area while safely diverting the current to the ground through a grounding system in the event of a lightning strike.

If lightning doesn’t strike the PDCE LIGHTNING PROTECTION SYSTEM, will it strike the house next door instead?

It is natural to worry that a strike suppressed by the PDCE LIGHTNING PROTECTION SYSTEM might hit the neighboring house instead.
However, in reality, lightning strikes occur simultaneously in multiple locations, and most dissipate before reaching the ground. Therefore, lightning diverted by the PDCE LIGHTNING PROTECTION SYSTEM will not necessarily strike a neighboring house.
Therefore, lightning diverted by the new PDCE lightning rod will not necessarily strike the neighboring house.
PDCE suppresses the upward leader that reaches the top of the structure and has no direct relationship with surrounding lightning strikes.
The electrical charge in thunderclouds is constantly changing, and lightning strikes are probabilistic. Since the energy is dissipated within the 20 to 40 minutes from the moment a strike occurs until it dissipates, preventing a strike with the PDCE LIGHTNING PROTECTION SYSTEM does not increase the number of strikes in the surrounding area.

Based on past examples, there has never been a single instance of trouble with neighbors resulting from the installation of the PDCE LIGHTNING PROTECTION SYSTEM.

Are a power source and ground connection required?

Power source
No power source is required. Only a connection to the ground is necessary.
The system utilizes the electrical charges generated on the ground. Induced by the negative charge at the base of a thundercloud, a positive charge accumulates on the ground directly beneath the cloud. This charge is then conducted through a ground wire to the PDCE LIGHTNING PROTECTION SYSTEM.

Ground connection
While it is desirable for the ground resistance to be as low as possible, unlike electrical safety grounding, there is no requirement that it must be 10 ohms or less.

Are consumables required? How much do maintenance costs amount to?

Consumables
Some competitors’ “lightning prevention devices” use salts to collect charges from the ground. Replenishing these salts in the ground every year can place a significant environmental burden.
It is also very costly. The PDCE LIGHTNING PROTECTION SYSTEM does not require the replenishment of salts or other materials.

Maintenance
Please perform a visual inspection once a year and measure the ground resistance every few years.

What are the advantages and disadvantages of the PDCE LIGHTNING PROTECTION SYSTEM?

Disadvantages

  1. Weighing between 2 kg and 10 kg, some models are heavy.
  2. The side surface area is larger than that of a simple rod, so wind pressure is greater.
  3. Because of points 1 and 2, the support tubes are thicker and heavier.

Advantages

  1. Because it does not attract lightning strikes, high currents do not flow through it, reducing adverse effects on a building’s ancillary equipment.
  2. Even if it cannot suppress lightning, it still functions like a conventional lightning rod by safely diverting the lightning current into the ground.
  3. Public expectations are changing. It is no longer enough to protect only one’s own facilities; it is also important to avoid adverse effects on surrounding areas and to help protect users of outdoor facilities such as events, amusement parks, golf courses, schools, and parks.
What is the protection range of the PDCE LIGHTNING PROTECTION SYSTEM?

Based on a comparison of laboratory results, installation records, and lightning strike data, we can say that the system provides protection with a very high probability within a 100-meter radius, and approximately five times the height of the PDCE unit.

Does the PDCE LIGHTNING PROTECTION SYSTEM get struck by lightning?

Since the PDCE is also a type of lightning rod, it can be struck by lightning under certain conditions. During winter thunderstorms or when thunderclouds are low, a PDCE installed at a high elevation may be covered by the thunderclouds, resulting in a definite discharge. Discharges can also occur between the PDCE and the ground when the electric field is extremely strong.

If it is struck by lightning, does it lose its effectiveness?
It will not function to suppress lightning, but it will still fulfill its basic function as a lightning protection device by diverting the lightning.

Will it be destroyed by a lightning strike?
Depending on the energy of the lightning, it may be destroyed. If struck directly near the top, the discharge will occur internally and flow to the ground; however, lightning does not always strike directly from above and may strike the side as well. For areas where direct strikes are expected, we offer the PDCE-Magnum, which features enhanced durability.

Is the PDCE LIGHTNING PROTECTION SYSTEM affected by weather and environmental conditions?

In addition to our products, there are other products designed to “prevent lightning strikes” that use a method of releasing electrical charges collected from the ground into the air to create a cloud of positive ions above the protected object. However, there is debate regarding whether these ions will remain above the protected object during rain or wind.

In contrast, our PDCE LIGHTNING PROTECTION SYSTEM is designed to charge the main unit with positive charges collected from the ground; it does not release ions into the air that envelop the entire protected structure. As a result, it is less susceptible to the effects of rain and wind.

Furthermore, if the upper and lower electrodes become covered by snow, an electrical connection is established between them, reducing the PDCE’s inherent lightning suppression effectiveness. However, even in such cases, it functions as a standard lightning rod, and it has been confirmed that its shape makes it less susceptible to being struck by lightning. Notably, during the winter season—when winter thunderstorms are frequent—there is typically a lot of dry snow, and as wet snow becomes more prevalent, the frequency of winter thunderstorms tends to decrease.

Reference: Characteristics of Winter Thunderclouds: Observation Results by Dr. Koichiro Michimoto, National Defense Academy of Japan

Observations have shown that the relationship between the altitude at which the air temperature reaches -10℃ and the ground temperature is important.

  1. When the thundercloud top is at -20°C and the altitude at -10°C is 1,800 m or higher, strong lightning is consistently observed.
  2. When the thundercloud top is at -20°C and the altitude at -10°C is 1,800 m or lower, only a single lightning strike occurs or no lightning occurs at all.
  3. When the thundercloud top is warmer than -20°C, lightning does not occur regardless of the altitude at -10°C.
  4. When the altitude at -10°C is 1,800 m or higher and the ground temperature is 3°C or higher, strong lightning strikes always occur.

The altitude at -10°C influences winter lightning, but during winter it is almost always below 3,000 m, and in the depths of winter, it is below 1,000 m. Observations show that winter thunderclouds occur at lower altitudes compared to summer cumulonimbus clouds.

Does the PDCE Lightning Protection System ever break?

With a standard lightning rod, the tip wears down slightly every time it is struck by lightning, and after repeated strikes, it eventually becomes nothing more than a plain rod. Installing a lightning protection device is not the end of the process; annual inspections are required.

We often receive the question, “Does the PDCE Lightning Protection System ever break?” However, when dealing with natural phenomena, nothing lasts forever.
In particular, during winter thunderstorms, the PDCE may become enveloped in the clouds at high altitudes, allowing discharges to occur.

While the insulation may be damaged by such unusual lightning strikes, the entire 360-degree circumference does not fail; the damage is limited to specific areas. The cause is not necessarily limited to the PDCE itself; factors such as whether adequate grounding was in place also play a role. However, the probability of such damage occurring is extremely low.

The PDCE LIGHTNING PROTECTION SYSTEM has been improved with a structure to prevent side discharges and optimized electrode connection methods to withstand even such unusual lightning strikes. Its strength has been verified through tensile tests of up to 10 tons, confirming that it will not separate.

If installed near an antenna, will it cause any interference or other issues?

Radar reflectors have a shape that is the opposite of the PDCE LIGHTNING PROTECTION SYSTEM. They are mounted on the masts of small vessels and consist of flat reflector panels designed to reflect incoming radar waves back in the direction they came from. The PDCE lightning protection device consists of two smooth hemispherical electrodes. It has undergone testing to obtain the European CE marking regarding electromagnetic compatibility and complies with the EMC Directive 2004/108/EC.

Can I verify the effectiveness of the PDCE lightning protection device after installation?
